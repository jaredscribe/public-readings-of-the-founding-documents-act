---

Rep. Mike Johnson  
Speaker of the House  
H-232, The Capitol  
Washington, DC 20515

Dear Mr. Speaker,

Tomorrow is Thursday, September 17 — Constitution Day — and the House is in session. I
am writing to ask you to recognize Members for a reading of the Constitution on the
floor of the House that day, and then to put the practice into the rules package that
opens the 120th Congress.

Your office is named in the precedent. On January 6, 2011, the House read the
Constitution aloud on its own floor under Section 5(a) of the rules package of the
112th Congress, which reads in full: "The Speaker may recognize a Member for the
reading of the Constitution on the legislative day of January 6, 2011."  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

The House has done it five times since — in the 112th, 113th, 114th, 115th, and 118th
Congresses — each time under an authorization that expired by its own terms within days
or weeks, each time at the opening of a Congress, and never once on the day that bears
the document's name. It has never been made a standing rule, and the Congress now
sitting has not authorized it at all.

Nothing about tomorrow requires a rules change. Time at the end of the day's
legislative business, conducted in turn by Members present, displaces no legislative
business, requires no appropriation, and needs no committee. It needs recognition from
the chair, which is yours to give, and your floor staff will know the cleanest vehicle
better than I do.

There is also the plain asymmetry between what Congress asks of the public and what it
asks of itself. Since December 2004, federal law has required every school receiving
federal funds to hold an educational program on the Constitution each September 17, and
every federal agency head to give each employee educational materials that day — Public
Law 108-447, Division J, Title I, Section 111. Congress directed the schoolteachers and
directed the executive branch, but directed nothing of itself — a basic failure of
intellectual and moral leadership, and of civic education. Why should we be surprised
at the failing schools and the anti-American universities?

Four requests:

1. **Tomorrow, the floor.** Recognize Members for a reading of the Constitution on
   September 17, voluntary and in succession.
2. **In January, the standing provision.** The House readopts its rules each Congress,
   so nothing adopted now outlives January 3, 2027, and permanence means the provision
   sits in the 120th Congress's opening-day rules package — which your office writes.
   Settling it before November is what keeps it from being a claim on an unknown
   majority. Public-domain model text:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md
3. **Next session, advance a statute.** It would require that the Constitution Day
   program schools already owe include a reading of the Constitution aloud to assembled
   students, and would ask state library agencies to describe in their plans how they
   will make Constitution Day materials available — materials only, with no new
   appropriation:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md  
   A companion bill would reach the federal agencies themselves, requiring each agency
   head to hold a reading before noon — of the Constitution on September 17, of the
   Declaration of Independence on July 4, and on Juneteenth of the Emancipation
   Proclamation, General Order No. 3, and the Thirteenth through Fifteenth Amendments:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a2-commemorative-days-observance-act.md
4. **Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order.** This year's Constitution Week
   proclamation has not yet issued and can be amended up to the hour it does. Every
   federal employee, starting at the Oval Office and the cabinet departments, should
   read the Constitution aloud with his or her coworkers on Constitution Day. A letter
   from the Speaker reaches the Domestic Policy Council in a way a private citizen like
   myself cannot:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

Every text above is released into the public domain — free to adopt, amend, rename, or
discard, with no permission needed and no credit owed. A reading requires no
appropriation, no new office, and no new employee: a microphone, willing readers, and
an hour.

I am asking the Democratic Leader for the same four things in the same week, and
saying so in both letters, because a practice adopted by one conference and abandoned
by the next is the problem; a bipartisan agreement on civic education and intellectual
leadership is the remedy.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig  
https://declareindependence.net
