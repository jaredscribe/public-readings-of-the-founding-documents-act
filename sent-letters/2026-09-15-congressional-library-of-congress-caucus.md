Washington, DC 20515

Dear Representative Aderholt and Representative Levin,

Constitution Day is Thursday, September 17, and the House is in session that day. I am
writing to ask the Library of Congress Caucus to press House leadership for a reading of
the Constitution on the floor that afternoon — and to say why this ask lands in your
caucus rather than another.

The Library of Congress maintains the *Constitution Annotated*, the government's own
authoritative exposition of the document, at https://constitution.congress.gov.
Congress built an institution to explain the Constitution to the country and has never
once set aside an hour of its own floor time to read it. Since December 2004 federal
law has required every federally funded school to hold a Constitution Day program and
every federal agency head to distribute educational materials that day — Public Law
108-447, Division J, Title I, Section 111 — and has required nothing of the body that
passed it.

There is a second reason. The model bill that follows this year's ask would carry the
Constitution Day observance into *public libraries*, through the state-plan assurances
attached to their federal grants — materials, not a required reading, and no library
obliged to hold an event. Libraries are your caucus's subject, and if that section is
going to be drafted well, it should be drafted with the people who know what a state
library agency can actually absorb:  
https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md  
A companion bill would reach the federal agencies themselves, requiring each agency
head to hold a reading before noon — of the Constitution on September 17, of the
Declaration of Independence on July 4, and on Juneteenth of the Emancipation
Proclamation, General Order No. 3, and the Thirteenth through Fifteenth Amendments:  
https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a2-commemorative-days-observance-act.md

Three immediate requests:

1. *Ask leadership for the floor on the 17th.* A reading conducted in turn on hte House
   floor by Members present displaces no legislative business and costs nothing, and
   sets the example of intellectual and moral leadership that we also ask of our
   nation's teachers.
2. *Deliver original co-sponsors for the standing rule in January*, since the House
   readopts its rules each Congress and permanence means the 120th Congress's
   opening-day package:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md
3. *If the floor does not happen, ask the Librarian to host it.* The Jefferson Building
   on Constitution Day, open to the public, would be the most natural location in
   Washington for a reading, and a request from this caucus is the one the Library
   would find hardest to refuse.

*Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order.* A letter from the caucus to the
Domestic Policy Council before this year's Constitution Week proclamation issues would
cost an hour and reach a desk at the White House that a private citizen like myself
reaches only by forwarding: Every federal employee should read the Constitution with
his or her co-workers on Sept 17th, starting in the Oval Office.  
https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

Every text is public domain — free to adopt, amend, rename, or discard.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig
