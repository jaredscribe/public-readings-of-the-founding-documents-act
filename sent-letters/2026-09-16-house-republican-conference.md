# Letter to Rep. Lisa McClain, Chair, House Republican Conference — as sent, 2026-09-16

**Submitted through Representative McClain's web contact form.** A web form returns no copy and no
confirmation, so this record is the campaign's drafting text at the moment of
submission, from which the plain-text submission was rendered. Markdown formatting is
kept here for legibility; the words are the words submitted.

**This copy is frozen.** The letter may be revised after it was sent; the current
version is published at <https://declareindependence.net/open-letters/house-republicans>.

---

Rep. Lisa McClain  
Chair, House Republican Conference  
562 Cannon House Office Building  
Washington, DC 20515

Dear Chairwoman McClain,

The House is in session this week through Thursday, and that Thursday is
September 17 — Constitution Day. I am writing to ask the Republican Conference to
put a reading of the Constitution on the floor of the House that day, and then to
make the practice permanent rather than leaving it to be granted again, or not, by
whoever holds the majority next.

Your conference began this practice. On January 6, 2011, the House read the
Constitution aloud on its own floor under Section 5(a) of the rules package of the
112th Congress, which read in full: "The Speaker may recognize a Member for the
reading of the Constitution on the legislative day of January 6, 2011." The House
has authorized such a reading five times since 2011 — in the 112th, 113th, 114th,
115th, and 118th Congresses. Every one of those authorizations wrote its own
expiration into the sentence that granted it: one legislative day in 2011, eleven
days in 2013, thirteen in 2015, ten in 2017, eight weeks in 2023. The text of the
first is here:  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

The House has never once made the practice a standing rule, and the Congress now
sitting has not authorized it at all. Every reading also occurred at the opening of
a Congress, in January or February — never on the day that bears the document's
name.

Meanwhile, since December 2004 federal law has required every school receiving
federal funds to hold an educational program on the Constitution each September 17,
and the head of every federal agency to give each employee educational materials on
that same day — Public Law 108-447, Division J, Title I, Section 111. Congress
directed the nation's schoolteachers to teach the Constitution and directed the
executive branch to instruct its own employees in it, but directed nothing whatever
of itself.

Four requests, in ascending order of effort:

1. **This Thursday.** The House is already scheduled to be in the room. A reading
   conducted in turn by Members present, each reading a portion in succession,
   displaces no legislative business and costs nothing. It needs a decision, not an
   appropriation.

2. **In January.** Because the House is not a continuing body and adopts its rules
   anew each Congress, no resolution passed now can outlive January 3, 2027. Real
   permanence means the provision goes into the 120th Congress's opening-day rules
   package — and the case for settling that now, before November, is that a
   practice written into the rules while its outcome is unknown to everyone is a
   practice that stops being anyone's property. Model resolution text, released
   into the public domain, free to adopt, amend, rename, or discard with no
   permission needed and no credit owed:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md

3. **Next session, advance a statute.** The larger version of the same idea would
   require that the Constitution Day educational program schools already owe
   include a reading of the Constitution aloud to assembled students, and would ask
   state library agencies to describe in their plans how they will make
   Constitution Day materials available — materials only, with no library required
   to hold anything, and no new appropriation, since materials the National
   Archives and the Library of Congress already produce would satisfy it. A model
   bill, on the same public-domain terms:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md  
   A companion bill would reach the federal agencies themselves, requiring each
   agency head to hold a reading before noon — of the Constitution on September 17,
   of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, and on Juneteenth of the
   Emancipation Proclamation, General Order No. 3, and the Thirteenth through
   Fifteenth Amendments:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a2-commemorative-days-observance-act.md

4. **Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order.** This year's Constitution
   Week proclamation has not yet been issued and can be amended up to the hour it
   is. One sentence calling on agency heads to hold readings would reach every
   federal employee at no cost. A letter from the Conference Chair to the Domestic
   Policy Council reaches a desk at the White House that a private citizen like
   myself reaches only by forwarding. The model executive order is drafted and
   public:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

The nation marked its 250th birthday this July. The Constitution's own 250th falls
on September 17, 2037. A practice begun this Thursday would be eleven years
established by then.

I have made the same request of the President, of the Archivist of the United
States, and of the Director of the Office of Personnel Management, and I am making
it of the Democratic Caucus in the same week — not as a courtesy, but because a
rule adopted by one party and repealed by the next is the problem; a bipartisan
agreement on civic education and intellectual leadership is the remedy.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig  
https://declareindependence.net
