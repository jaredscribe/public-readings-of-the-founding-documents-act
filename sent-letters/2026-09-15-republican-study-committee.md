Rep. August Pfluger  
Chairman, Republican Study Committee  
U.S. House of Representatives  
Washington, DC 20515

Dear Chairman Pfluger,

Constitution Day is Thursday, September 17, and the House is in session that day. I am
writing to ask the Republican Study Committee to press for a reading of the
Constitution on the House floor that afternoon, and then to make the practice a
standing rule — while your conference still holds the pen that writes the rules
package.

This practice is yours. The House first read the Constitution aloud on its own floor on
January 6, 2011, under Section 5(a) of the rules package of the 112th Congress: "The
Speaker may recognize a Member for the reading of the Constitution on the legislative
day of January 6, 2011."  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

It has been authorized five times since — 112th, 113th, 114th, 115th, 118th — each
time with an expiration written into the granting sentence, and each time by a
Republican majority. It has never been made a standing rule, and the Congress now
sitting has not authorized it at all. Fifteen years of choosing the practice, and not
one of keeping it.

That is worth saying bluntly: a commitment re-granted five times and never once made
permanent is a commitment held at the pleasure of whoever next holds the gavel. The
window to fix it is this Congress, and the argument for fixing it before November is
that a practice that must be re-adopted by each incoming majority is a practice that
will eventually lapse — and it already has.

There is also the plain inconsistency. Since December 2004, federal law has required
every school receiving federal funds to hold an educational program on the Constitution
each September 17, and every federal agency head to give each employee educational
materials that day — Public Law 108-447, Division J, Title I, Section 111. Congress
directed the nation's teachers to teach the Constitution and directed the executive
branch to instruct its employees in it, but directed nothing whatever of itself. A body
that requires civic instruction of everyone but itself has more than just a messaging
problem. Congress should practice the intellectual and moral leadership that it asks of
the nation's teachers - or else has no cause for surprise and outrage at failing
schools and anti-American ideologies.

Four requests:

1. The floor, this Thursday. A reading conducted in turn by Members present on the
   House floor, displaces almost no legislative business and requires no
   appropriation. If an hour on the House floor cannot be obtained, join me on the
   Capitol steps to read the Constitution aloud to the assembled public.
2. The standing rule, into the 120th Congress's opening-day package. The House
   readopts its rules each Congress, so nothing adopted now outlives January 3, 2027,
   unless a standing rule makes this a permanent tradition:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md
3. Next session, advance a statute. The educational program schools already owe on
   September 17 would have to include a reading of the Constitution aloud to assembled
   students, and state library agencies would describe in their plans how they will
   make Constitution Day materials available — materials only, no library required to
   hold anything, no new program, and no new appropriation, since materials the
   National Archives and the Library of Congress already produce would satisfy it:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md  
   A companion bill would reach the federal agencies themselves, requiring each agency
   head to hold a reading before noon — of the Constitution on September 17, of the
   Declaration of Independence on July 4, and on Juneteenth of the Emancipation
   Proclamation, General Order No. 3, and the Thirteenth through Fifteenth Amendments:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a2-commemorative-days-observance-act.md
4. Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order. This year's Constitution Week
   proclamation has not issued and can be amended up to the hour it is. Every federal
   employee should read the Constitution on Sept 17th with his or her co-workers,
   starting in the Oval Office. A letter from the Chairman to the Domestic Policy
   Council reaches a desk at the White House that a private citizen like myself reaches
   only by forwarding:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

Every text above is released into the public domain — free to adopt, amend, rename, or
discard, with no permission needed and no credit owed.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig
