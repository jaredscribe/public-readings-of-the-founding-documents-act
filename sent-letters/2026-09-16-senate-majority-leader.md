# Letter to Sen. John Thune, Majority Leader — as sent, 2026-09-16

**Submitted through Senator Thune's web contact form.** A web form returns no copy and no
confirmation, so this record is the campaign's drafting text at the moment of
submission, from which the plain-text submission was rendered. Markdown formatting is
kept here for legibility; the words are the words submitted.

**This copy is frozen.** The letter may be revised after it was sent; the current
version is published at <https://declareindependence.net/open-letters/senate-leader>.

---

Sen. John Thune  
Majority Leader  
SD-511 Dirksen Senate Office Building  
Washington, DC 20510

Dear Leader Thune,

The Senate is in session this week, through Friday the
18th. Thursday the 17th is Constitution Day. I am writing
to ask that the Senate read the Constitution aloud on its floor that day, and I
am writing to you because you are the only person I am writing to this week who
controls whether the request is ever offered.

The instrument is a unanimous consent agreement — no committee, no report, no vote.
It costs the Senate an hour of a day it is already scheduled to be in session, and it
can still be done before Thursday. I am asking Leader Schumer in the same week not to
object, and asking Senator Cotton to carry it in the conference.

The mechanics are not speculative. The House has read the Constitution aloud on its
own floor five times since 2011 — in the 112th, 113th, 114th, 115th, and 118th
Congresses — conducted in turn by Members present, each reading a portion in
succession, without disrupting the legislative schedule. The first authorization
reads in full: "The Speaker may recognize a Member for the reading of the
Constitution on the legislative day of January 6, 2011."  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

**In fifteen years neither chamber has made the practice standing, and the Senate is
the chamber that can.** Each of those five House authorizations expired by its own
terms, within days or weeks; the Congress now sitting granted none. And because the
House readopts its rules at the opening of every Congress, anything it adopts has to
be adopted again two years later. The Senate's standing orders carry forward without
readoption. A standing order establishing this reading would be the first permanent
one in either chamber — and it would belong to the Senate.

There is also the matter of what Congress has required of everyone but itself. Since
December 2004, federal law has obliged every school receiving federal funds to hold
an educational program on the Constitution each September 17, and the head of every
federal agency to give each employee educational materials that day — Public Law
108-447, Division J, Title I, Section 111. Congress directed the nation's teachers
and directed the executive branch, but directed nothing whatever of itself.

Four requests, in ascending order of effort:

1. **Let it be propounded this Thursday.** A unanimous consent agreement for a
   reading of the Constitution on September 17, conducted in turn by Senators
   present. An illustrative form, which Senate legislative counsel and the cloakrooms
   would of course put into the chamber's own idiom:

   *"I ask unanimous consent that, following leader remarks on Thursday,
   September 17, the Senate proceed to the reading of the Constitution of the United
   States, including its amendments, by Senators present, each reading such portion
   as the Chair may assign, in succession until the reading is complete; and that the
   reading be printed in the Record."*

2\. **Then a standing order, so it recurs.** A unanimous consent agreement dies on a
   single objection, which makes it cheap to grant and fragile to rely on — one
   Senator should not be able to cancel the reading in a future year. This campaign
   is expressly **not** asking the Senate to amend its Standing Rules; a standing
   order delivers everything the practice needs and amends none of them. The House
   model resolution shows the shape of the operative text, though a Senate order
   would be considerably shorter:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md

3\. **Next session, advance a statute.** It would require that the Constitution Day
   educational program schools already owe include a reading of the Constitution
   aloud to assembled students, and would ask state library agencies to describe in
   their plans how they will make Constitution Day materials available — materials
   only, with no library required to hold anything, and no new appropriation, since
   materials already produced by the National Archives and the Library of Congress
   would satisfy it:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md  
   A companion bill would reach the federal agencies themselves, requiring each
   agency head to hold a reading before noon — of the Constitution on September 17,
   of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, and on Juneteenth of the
   Emancipation Proclamation, General Order No. 3, and the Thirteenth through
   Fifteenth Amendments:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a2-commemorative-days-observance-act.md

4\. **Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order.** This year's Constitution
   Week proclamation has not been issued and can be amended up to the hour it is. One
   sentence calling on agency heads to hold readings would reach every federal
   employee at no cost. A letter from the Majority Leader to the Domestic Policy
   Council reaches a desk at the White House that a private citizen like myself
   reaches only by forwarding. The model executive order:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

Every text above is released into the public domain — free to adopt, amend, rename,
or discard, with no permission needed and no credit owed. Nothing has to be drafted.
A reading requires no appropriation, no new office, and no new employee: a
microphone, willing readers, and an hour.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig  
https://declareindependence.net
