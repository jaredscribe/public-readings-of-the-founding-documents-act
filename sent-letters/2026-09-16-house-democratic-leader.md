# Letter to Rep. Hakeem Jeffries, Democratic Leader — as sent, 2026-09-16

**Submitted through the Democratic Leader's web contact form.** A web form returns no copy and no
confirmation, so this record is the campaign's drafting text at the moment of
submission, from which the plain-text submission was rendered. Markdown formatting is
kept here for legibility; the words are the words submitted.

**This copy is frozen.** The letter may be revised after it was sent; the current
version is published at <https://declareindependence.net/open-letters/house-democratic-leader>.

---

Rep. Hakeem Jeffries  
Democratic Leader  
2267 Rayburn House Office Building  
Washington, DC 20515

Dear Leader Jeffries,

Tomorrow is Thursday, September 17 — Constitution Day — and the House is in session. I
am writing to ask you to join the request for a reading of the Constitution on the
House floor that day, and to ask you for something the majority cannot supply: that the
practice stop being one party's.

It is one party's now, by accident rather than by anything in the document. The House
has authorized a reading of the Constitution on its own floor five times since 2011 —
in the 112th, 113th, 114th, 115th, and 118th Congresses — each time inside an
opening-day rules package, each time with an expiration written into the granting
sentence, beginning with Section 5(a) of the 112th Congress's rules: "The Speaker may
recognize a Member for the reading of the Constitution on the legislative day of
January 6, 2011."  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

Every one of those authorizations came from a Republican majority. The practice has
never been made standing, it lapsed in the Congresses between, and the Congress now
sitting has not authorized it at all. So reading the founding charter has drifted into
something one party grants itself and the other declines to — a strange fate for the
instrument by which every Member holds office and to which every Member swears an oath.

**A standing rule is what ends that, and only the party that did not start the practice
can end its partisan character.** If the Speaker grants the floor tomorrow at the
request of both leaders, the reading belongs to the House. If the provision goes into
the 120th Congress's opening-day rules package with Democratic co-sponsors on it, it
survives whichever party writes that package.

There is a second asymmetry worth naming. Since December 2004, federal law has required
every federally funded school to hold a Constitution Day program and every federal
agency head to hand each employee educational materials that day — Public Law 108-447,
Division J, Title I, Section 111. Congress directed the schoolteachers and directed the
executive branch, but directed nothing of itself. A legislature that requires civic
instruction of everyone but itself teaches by that omission more than it intends.

Four requests:

1. **Tomorrow, join the ask.** Support a reading on the floor on September 17,
   conducted in turn by Members present, voluntary, displacing no legislative business.
   If the floor does not happen, the caucus can hold the reading itself — a committee
   room or the Capitol steps on the 17th, open to the press, makes the floor version
   obviously possible next year.
2. **In January, deliver original co-sponsors for the standing rule.** The House is not
   a continuing body; nothing adopted now outlives January 3, 2027, so permanence means
   the 120th Congress's opening-day package, and asking before November — while neither
   party knows who will write it — is the only moment it can be asked for on the merits.
   Public-domain model text:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md
3. **Next session, advance a statute.** It would require that the Constitution Day
   program schools already owe include a reading of the Constitution aloud to assembled
   students, and would ask state library agencies to describe in their plans how they
   will make Constitution Day materials available — materials only, with no new
   appropriation:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md  
   A companion bill would reach the federal agencies themselves, requiring each agency
   head to hold a reading before noon — of the Constitution on September 17, of the
   Declaration of Independence on July 4, and on Juneteenth of the Emancipation
   Proclamation, General Order No. 3, and the Thirteenth through Fifteenth Amendments:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a2-commemorative-days-observance-act.md
4. **Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order.** This year's Constitution Week
   proclamation has not yet issued and can be amended up to the hour it does. One
   sentence calling on agency heads to hold readings would reach every federal employee
   at no cost:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

Every text above is released into the public domain — free to adopt, amend, rename, or
discard, with no permission needed and no credit owed.

I am asking the Speaker for the same four things in the same week, and I have written
to the Democratic Caucus through Chairman Aguilar. A practice adopted by one conference
and abandoned by the next is the problem; a bipartisan agreement on civic education and
intellectual leadership is the remedy.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig  
https://declareindependence.net
