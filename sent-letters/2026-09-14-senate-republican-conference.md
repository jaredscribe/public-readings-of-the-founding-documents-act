# Letter to Sen. Tom Cotton, Chair, Senate Republican Conference — as sent, 2026-09-14

**Sent 2026-09-14, 19:45 UTC, by email to the Senator's press office**, the only address
his site publishes, with a short preface asking that it be passed to whoever handles
floor and policy matters. This is the text the office received, transcribed from the
sent message. Line wrapping and list formatting were restored from the plain-text part
of the email; the words are unchanged. The preface is omitted.

**This copy is frozen.** The letter has been revised since it was sent; the current
version is published at <https://declareindependence.net/open-letters/senate-republicans>.

**One error in the sent text, left as sent:** request 3 says the schools-and-libraries
bill "also requires readings of the Declaration on July 4th Independence day, and the
Emancipation proclamation and 13th-15th amendments on Juneteenth." It does not. Those
readings are in a separate companion bill, the Commemorative Days Observance Act, which
binds federal agencies rather than schools — and its Juneteenth readings also include
General Order No. 3. The current version describes the two bills separately.

Sen. Tom Cotton  
Chair, Senate Republican Conference  
326 Russell Senate Office Building  
Washington, DC 20510

Dear Senator Cotton,

The Senate is in session this week, through Friday the 18th. Thursday the 17th is
Constitution Day. I am writing to ask that the Senate read the Constitution aloud on
its floor that day and then make the practice standing.

The mechanics are not speculative, and the Senate does not have to invent them. The
House has read the Constitution aloud on its floor five times since 2011 — in the
112th, 113th, 114th, 115th, and 118th Congresses — conducted in turn by Members
present, each reading a portion in succession, without disrupting the legislative
schedule. The first authorization is public and reads in full: "The Speaker may
recognize a Member for the reading of the Constitution on the legislative day of
January 6, 2011."  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

*And in fifteen years the House has never once made it permanent.* Every one of those
five authorizations carried its own expiration inside the sentence that granted it —
one legislative day in 2011, then eleven days, thirteen, ten, eight weeks. The Congress
now sitting authorized none at all. Nor can the House settle the question once: it is
not a continuing body, it readopts its rules at the opening of every Congress, so
anything it adopts has to be adopted again two years later.

*The Senate can. That is the whole of my request.* Because the Senate is a continuing
body, its standing orders carry forward from Congress to Congress without readoption. A
standing order establishing this reading would be the first permanent one in either
chamber — not merely first in time, but the only permanent version a chamber's own
rules can provide.

Two steps, and the first does not depend on the second:

1. *A unanimous consent agreement for this year.* It needs no committee, no report,
and no vote — a Senator propounds it on the floor and, absent objection, it is agreed
to. It could put a reading on the floor this Thursday. I will name its weakness before
your staff has to: a unanimous consent agreement dies on a single objection, and one
Senator could cancel the reading in any future year. An illustrative form, which Senate
legislative counsel and the cloakrooms would of course put into the chamber's own
idiom:

*"I ask unanimous consent that, following leader remarks on Thursday, September 17, the
Senate proceed to the reading of the Constitution of the United States, including its
amendments, by Senators present, each reading such portion as the Chair may assign, in
succession until the reading is complete; and that the reading be printed in the
Record."*

2. *A standing order to make it recur.* That fragility is the argument for the second
step, and the two together are the same ladder this campaign has put to the executive
branch — the cheap instrument does it this year, the durable one makes it permanent.
The House model resolution shows the shape of the operative text, though a Senate
standing order would be shorter and would not touch the standing rules, which this
campaign is expressly not asking the Senate to amend:  
https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md

Two further requests, both smaller than they sound:

3. *Next session, advance a statute.* Since December 2004 federal law has required
every federally funded school to hold a Constitution Day program, and every federal
agency head to hand each employee educational materials that day — Public Law 108-447,
Division J, Title I, Section 111. Congress directed the schoolteachers and directed the
executive branch, but directed nothing of itself. The bill would require that the
program schools already owe include a reading of the Constitution aloud to assembled
students, and would ask state library agencies to describe in their plans how they will
make Constitution Day materials available — materials only, with no library required
to hold anything, and no new appropriation. It also requires readings of the
Declaration on July 4th Independence day, and the Emancipation proclamation and
13th-15th amendments on Juneteenth. Schools and libraries; federal agencies are the
executive branch's own business. Model text, public domain.  
https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md

4. *Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order.* This year's Constitution Week
proclamation has not been issued and can be amended up to the hour it is. One sentence
calling on agency heads to hold readings would reach every federal employee at no cost.
A letter from the Conference Chair to the Domestic Policy Council reaches a desk at the
White House that a private citizen like myself reaches only by forwarding. The model
executive order:  
https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

Every text above is released into the public domain — free to adopt, amend, rename, or
discard, with no permission needed and no credit owed. Nothing has to be drafted. A
reading requires no appropriation, no new office, and no new employee: a microphone,
willing readers, and an hour.

I am making the same request of the Majority Leader, whose floor staff would have to
clear it, and of the Democratic Leader in the same week — because a unanimous consent
agreement requires that no one object, and because a practice adopted by one
conference and abandoned by the next is the problem; a bipartisan agreement on civic
education and intellectual leadership, is the remedy.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig  
https://declareindependence.net
