# Letter to Rep. Pete Aguilar, Chair, House Democratic Caucus — as sent, 2026-09-16

**Submitted through Representative Aguilar's web contact form.** A web form returns no copy and no
confirmation, so this record is the campaign's drafting text at the moment of
submission, from which the plain-text submission was rendered. Markdown formatting is
kept here for legibility; the words are the words submitted.

**This copy is frozen.** The letter may be revised after it was sent; the current
version is published at <https://declareindependence.net/open-letters/house-democrats>.

---

Rep. Pete Aguilar  
Chair, House Democratic Caucus  
1420 Longworth House Office Building  
Washington, DC 20515

Dear Chairman Aguilar,

The House is in session this week through Thursday, and that Thursday is
September 17 — Constitution Day. I am writing to ask the Democratic Caucus to
support putting a reading of the Constitution on the floor of the House that day,
and to make it a standing practice rather than a partisan one.

It is a partisan one now, and that is an accident of how it began rather than
anything in the document. The House has authorized a reading of the Constitution on
its own floor five times since 2011 — in the 112th, 113th, 114th, 115th, and 118th
Congresses. Each authorization sat inside an opening-day rules package with an
expiration written into the sentence itself, beginning with Section 5(a) of the
112th Congress's rules: "The Speaker may recognize a Member for the reading of the
Constitution on the legislative day of January 6, 2011."  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

The practice was instituted by a Republican majority and has tracked that majority
since. It has never been made a standing rule, it lapsed in the Congresses between,
and the Congress now sitting has not authorized it at all. So the reading of the
founding charter has become something one party grants itself and the other
declines to — a strange fate for the instrument by which every Member holds office
and to which every Member swears an oath.

**A standing rule is what ends that, and it can only be ended by the party that did
not start it.** If the Republican Conference alone makes the practice permanent, it
stays theirs. If the Democratic Caucus asks for the same thing in the same week, it
stops being anyone's.

There is a second asymmetry worth naming. Since December 2004, federal law has
required every federally funded school to hold a Constitution Day program and every
federal agency head to hand each employee educational materials that day — Public
Law 108-447, Division J, Title I, Section 111. Congress directed the schoolteachers
and directed the executive branch, but directed nothing of itself. A legislature
that requires civic instruction of others and undertakes none in its own chamber
teaches by that omission more than it intends.

Four requests, in ascending order of effort:

1. **This Thursday.** Support a reading on the floor on September 17, conducted in
   turn by Members present. Participation is voluntary; no Member is required to
   read, and no Member's absence affects it. It displaces no legislative business
   and requires no appropriation.

2. **In January.** The House is not a continuing body — it readopts its rules each
   Congress, so nothing passed now survives January 3, 2027. Permanence means
   getting the provision into the 120th Congress's opening-day rules package, and
   asking for that *before* November, while neither party knows who will be writing
   that package, is the only moment at which it can be asked for on the merits.
   Public-domain model text, free to adopt, amend, rename or discard:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md

3. **Next session, advance a statute.** Schools already owe an educational program
   on September 17; in practice it is usually a handout. The bill would require that
   program to include a reading of the Constitution aloud to assembled students,
   and would ask state library agencies to describe in their plans how they will
   make Constitution Day materials available — materials, not a required reading,
   and no new appropriation. A model bill:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md  
   A companion bill would reach the federal agencies themselves, requiring each
   agency head to hold a reading before noon — of the Constitution on September 17,
   of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, and on Juneteenth of the
   Emancipation Proclamation, General Order No. 3, and the Thirteenth through
   Fifteenth Amendments:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a2-commemorative-days-observance-act.md

4. **Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order.** This year's Constitution
   Week proclamation has not issued yet. One sentence in it calling on agency heads
   to hold readings would reach every federal employee and cost nothing. A letter
   from the Caucus Chair to the Domestic Policy Council reaches a desk at the White
   House that a private citizen like myself reaches only by forwarding. The model
   executive order is drafted and public:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

I am asking the Republican Conference for exactly the same four things in the same
week, and saying so in both letters. A practice adopted by one party and abandoned
by the next is the problem; a bipartisan agreement on civic education and
intellectual leadership is the remedy.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig  
https://declareindependence.net
