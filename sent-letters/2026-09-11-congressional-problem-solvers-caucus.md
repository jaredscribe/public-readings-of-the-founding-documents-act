Dear Representative Fitzpatrick and Representative Suozzi,

Constitution Day is Thursday, September 17, and the House is in session Monday
through Thursday of next week. I am writing with a request that is, I think,
unusually well matched to your caucus: put a reading of the Constitution on the
House floor that day, and make it a standing rule.

It costs nothing. It requires no appropriation, no new office, no new employee, and
no floor time that displaces legislative business — a reading conducted in turn by
Members present, each reading a portion in succession, participation voluntary. And
it is the rare procedural change with no partisan valence available to it, since the
document belongs to both parties and every Member swears an oath to it.

**Except that in practice it has acquired one, and that is the actual problem to
solve.** The House has authorized a reading of the Constitution on its own floor
five times since 2011 — in the 112th, 113th, 114th, 115th, and 118th Congresses.
Every one of those authorizations carried its expiration inside the sentence that
granted it, starting with Section 5(a) of the 112th Congress's rules package: "The
Speaker may recognize a Member for the reading of the Constitution on the legislative
day of January 6, 2011."  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

The practice was instituted by a Republican majority and has tracked that majority
ever since. It has never been made standing, it lapsed in the Congresses between, and
the Congress now sitting has not authorized it at all. So reading the founding
charter has become something one party grants itself and the other declines to — a
purely procedural accident with an entirely avoidable result.

A bipartisan caucus is the only body that can fix that, and the fix is cheap:

1. **Ask both conferences for the floor on September 17**, jointly and in the same
   week. The ask from a Republican and a Democrat together is the entire point.
2. **Carry the standing rule into January.** The House is not a continuing body — it
   readopts its rules each Congress, so nothing passed now outlives January 3, 2027,
   and permanence means the provision sits in the 120th Congress's opening-day rules
   package. Asking for it *before* November, when no one yet knows who will write
   that package, is the only moment at which it can be asked for on the merits.
   Public-domain model text:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md
3. **If the floor does not happen, hold it yourselves** on the Capitol steps on the
   17th, co-chairs reading first and alternating by party down the list - for the
   benefit and education of the general public. That image is the argument. I will
   be there to read it myself, if no one else will.

Two smaller things. A future-session bill would require that the Constitution Day
educational program schools already owe include a reading of the Constitution aloud
to assembled students, and would reach public libraries as materials only, through a
line in the state plans their federal grants already require —  
https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md

— and a letter from the caucus to the Domestic Policy Council, before this year's
Constitution Week proclamation issues, would cost a staffer an hour and reach a desk
no private citizen reaches:  
https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

Every text is public domain — free to adopt, amend, rename, or discard, with no
permission needed and no credit owed. Nothing has to be drafted.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig
