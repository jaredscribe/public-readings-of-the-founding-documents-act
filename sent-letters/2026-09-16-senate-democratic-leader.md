# Letter to Sen. Chuck Schumer, Democratic Leader — as sent, 2026-09-16

**Submitted through Senator Schumer's web contact form.** A web form returns no copy and no
confirmation, so this record is the campaign's drafting text at the moment of
submission, from which the plain-text submission was rendered. Markdown formatting is
kept here for legibility; the words are the words submitted.

**This copy is frozen.** The letter may be revised after it was sent; the current
version is published at <https://declareindependence.net/open-letters/senate-democrats>.

---

Sen. Chuck Schumer  
Democratic Leader  
322 Hart Senate Office Building  
Washington, DC 20510

Dear Leader Schumer,

The Senate is in session this week, through Friday the 18th.
Thursday the 17th is Constitution Day. I am writing to ask that the Senate read the
Constitution aloud on its floor that day, and to ask you for something more
specific than support: **that your conference not object.**

The instrument that can still do this before Thursday is a unanimous consent
agreement. It needs no committee, no report, and no vote. It also dies on a single
objection — which means the request I am making of the Majority Leader and of the
Republican Conference Chair this week cannot succeed without you, and that you hold
a decision here that is not usually yours to hold.

The mechanics are proven. The House has read the Constitution aloud on its floor
five times since 2011 — in the 112th, 113th, 114th, 115th, and 118th Congresses —
each time conducted in turn by Members present, without disrupting the
legislative schedule. The first authorization reads in full: "The Speaker may
recognize a Member for the reading of the Constitution on the legislative day of
January 6, 2011."  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

**But neither chamber has ever made the practice standing, and only one of them can
settle it once.** Each of the five House authorizations expired by its own terms
within days or weeks, and the Congress now sitting granted none. The House is not a
continuing body — it readopts its rules at the opening of every Congress, so anything
it adopts must be adopted again two years later. The Senate's standing orders carry
forward without readoption. A Senate standing order establishing this reading would
be the first permanent one in either chamber.

There is a reason to want that which is not symbolic. Instituted by one majority
and tracked to it ever since, the practice of reading the founding charter has
drifted into being one party's property — granted when that party holds the gavel,
lapsed when it does not. **A standing order, agreed to by both conferences, is what
takes the Constitution back out of that column.** It is a poor arrangement in which
reading the document that every Senator swears an oath to support is understood as
a partisan gesture, and it is the minority's agreement, far more than the
majority's, that would end it.

Four requests:

1. **Do not object, this Thursday.** If a unanimous consent agreement is propounded
   for a reading of the Constitution on September 17, let it be agreed to. That
   alone is the whole of the near-term ask, and it costs the Senate an hour of a
   day it is already scheduled to be in session.

2. **Co-sponsor the standing order.** A unanimous consent agreement one Senator can
   cancel next year is not a practice; a standing order is. The House model text
   shows the shape of the operative provisions, though a Senate standing order would
   be shorter and would not touch the standing rules, which this campaign expressly
   does not ask the Senate to amend:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md

3. **Next session, advance a statute.** Since December 2004, federal law has required
   every federally funded school to hold a Constitution Day program and every
   federal agency head to give each employee educational materials that day —
   Public Law 108-447, Division J, Title I, Section 111. Congress directed the
   schoolteachers and directed the executive branch, but directed nothing of itself.
   The bill would require that the program schools already owe include a reading of
   the Constitution aloud to assembled students, and would ask state library agencies
   to describe in their plans how they will make Constitution Day materials
   available — materials only, with no library required to hold anything, and no new
   appropriation.  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md  
   A companion bill would reach the federal agencies themselves, requiring each
   agency head to hold a reading before noon — of the Constitution on September 17,
   of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, and on Juneteenth of the
   Emancipation Proclamation, General Order No. 3, and the Thirteenth through
   Fifteenth Amendments:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a2-commemorative-days-observance-act.md

4. **Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order.** This year's Constitution
   Week proclamation has not issued and can be amended up to the hour it is. One
   sentence calling on agency heads to hold readings would reach every federal
   employee at no cost. A letter from the Democratic Leader to the Domestic Policy
   Council reaches a desk at the White House that a private citizen like myself
   reaches only by forwarding.  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

Every text above is released into the public domain — free to adopt, amend, rename,
or discard, with no permission needed and no credit owed.

A practice adopted by one conference and abandoned by the next is the problem; a
bipartisan agreement on civic education and intellectual leadership is the remedy.

Respectfully,

Jared Essig  
https://declareindependence.net
