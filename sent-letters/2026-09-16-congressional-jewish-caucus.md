Administration lists for the caucus co-chairs.** This is the text the office received,
transcribed from the sent message. Line wrapping and list formatting were restored from

**This copy is frozen.** The letter has been revised since it was sent; the current

---

Rep. Jerrold Nadler and Rep. Brad Schneider  
Co-Chairs, Congressional Jewish Caucus  
U.S. House of Representatives  
Washington, DC 20515

Dear Representative Nadler and Representative Schneider,

Constitution Day is this Thursday, September 17, and the House is in session that day.
I am writing to ask the Congressional Jewish Caucus to press for a reading of the
Constitution on the House floor that morning or afternoon, then to make the practice a
standing rule, and furthermore to sponsor the legislation that would carry this
tradition into the nation's schools and federal agencies. This request belongs to your
caucus in particular.

We already know how a people keeps its founding - through the command to *remember*.
On the first day of Passover we read the going-out from Egypt, Exodus 12:21–51. On the
first day of Shavuot we read the covenant at Sinai, Exodus 19:1–20:23. The text of the
event is read aloud, in public, on the anniversary of the event, every year, and the
days counted between the two carry us from liberation to law.

שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר "זָכוֹר אֶת־הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה אֲשֶׁר יְצָאתֶם מִמִּצְרַיִם" (שמות יג, ג), וְגַם "וְהוֹדַעְתָּם לְבָנֶיךָ וְלִבְנֵי בָנֶיךָ — יוֹם אֲשֶׁר עָמַדְתָּ לִפְנֵי ה' אֱלֹהֶיךָ בְּחֹרֵב" (דברים ד, ט–י) — "Remember this day, on which you went free from
Egypt"; "make them known to your children and to your children's children — the day
you stood before the LORD your God at Horeb."The Torah further specifies these
principles of remembrance by making the public reading itself a commandment: "Gather
the people—men, women, children, and the strangers in your communities—that they may
hear and so learn" (Deuteronomy 31:12). And it asks the same of the one who governs,
who is to keep a copy of the holy Law and "read in it all his life" (Deuteronomy
17:19).

The American calendar has the same shape — liberation in 1776, law in 1787 — and
almost none of the practice. Since December 2004, federal law has required every
school receiving federal funds to hold an educational program on the Constitution each
September 17, and every federal agency head to give each employee educational
materials that day — Public Law 108-447, Division J, Title I, Section 111. Congress
directed the schoolteachers and directed the executive branch, but directed nothing of
itself. The House has read the Constitution aloud on its floor five times since 2011
(in the 112th, 113th, 114th, 115th, and 118th Congresses). Each time occurred at the
opening of a Congress under an authorization that expired within days or weeks; it was
never a standing rule, never occurred on Constitution Day. The Congress now sitting
has not authorized it at all. The first authorization reads in full: "The Speaker may
recognize a Member for the reading of the Constitution on the legislative day of
January 6, 2011."  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-112hres5eh/html/BILLS-112hres5eh.htm

Each of those five authorizations came from a Republican majority, and so a reading
of the document every Member swears to support has come to look like one party's
gesture. When your caucus was founded, it described itself as "a bridge to other
members and organizations within the House." I am asking the Republican Study
Committee for the same reading this week. Jewish Members asking for it too is how it
stops belonging to either party. This year Constitution Day falls between Rosh Hashanah
and Yom Kippur, in the days Jews set aside for taking stock, making a personal and
national moral inventory, confession, and recommitment to founding principles: another
tradition from which America could benefit.

Four requests:

1. *This Thursday, ask for the floor.* A reading conducted in turn by Members present
   is voluntary, displaces no legislative business, and requires no appropriation. If
   the floor reading doesn't happen, hold the reading yourselves—in a committee room,
   or with me on the Capitol steps on the 17th, open to the public and the press.
2. *In January, deliver original co-sponsors for the standing rule.* The House
   readopts its rules each Congress, so nothing adopted now outlives January 3, 2027,
   and permanence means the provision goes into the 120th Congress's opening-day rules
   package. Public-domain model text:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-b-chamber-rule-resolution.md
3. *Next session, sponsor the statute.* The first bill would require that the
   Constitution Day program schools already owe include a reading of the Constitution
   aloud to assembled students, and would ask state library agencies to describe in
   their plans how they will make Constitution Day materials available — materials
   only, with no new appropriation:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a-public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act.md  
   A companion bill would reach the federal agencies themselves, requiring each agency
   head to hold a reading before noon — of the Constitution on September 17, of the
   Declaration of Independence on July 4, and on Juneteenth of the Emancipation
   Proclamation, General Order No. 3, and the Thirteenth through Fifteenth Amendments.
   Juneteenth marks an American going-out from bondage; on it, as on Passover, the
   texts of that freedom should be read aloud:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-a2-commemorative-days-observance-act.md
4. *Now, lobby the White House for an Executive Order.* This year's Constitution Week
   proclamation has not yet issued and can be amended up to the hour it does. Every
   federal employee in every bureau and agency, should read the document that
   authorizes their employment - starting in the Oval office and cabinet departments.
   The press secretary could invite the white house press corps to read the
   Constitution aloud in the briefing room before answering any of their questions at
   the next press conference. A letter from the caucus to the Domestic Policy Council
   calling for this, would cost a staffer an hour and reach a desk at the White House
   that a private citizen like myself reaches only by forwarding:  
   https://github.com/jaredscribe/public-readings-of-the-founding-documents-act/blob/main/track-c-executive-order.md

We were charged to be לְאוֹר גּוֹיִם, "a light of the nations" (Isaiah 42:6, 49:6), and the
very next verse says what the light is for: "To bring out the prisoners from the
dungeon, and them that sit in darkness out of the prison-house" (42:7, 49:9). Isaiah
later encourages us: "Arise, shine, for thy light is come, and the glory of the LORD is
risen upon thee… And nations shall walk at thy light, and kings at the brightness of
thy rising" (60:1, 3). That charge doesn't ask us to be less American.

It asks us to work for the good of this country of our exile and to pray for its
welfare — the practice Jeremiah commanded the exiles in Babylon, in a letter of his
own: "And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away
captive, and pray unto the LORD for it; for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace"
(Jeremiah 29:7). So let us show America what a people does with the texts that made
it. A caucus that brings our oldest civic practice — reading the founding text aloud,
on its day, before the whole assembly — to the floor of the House would fulfill a
significant part of its Jewish mission and its American one in the same act.

Every text above is released into the public domain — free to adopt, amend, rename, or
discard, with no permission needed and no credit owed.

Gmar chatima tovah,

May we be written in the book of life, of wisdom, of counsel, and of strength, together
with all Israel,

Jared Essig
