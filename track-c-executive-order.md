# Track C — Model Executive Order

*Model executive order text. Working draft — see the [status note](README.md#status-working-draft-not-reviewed-by-counsel).*

**Restoring the Public Reading of the Founding Documents**

The President acting on the executive branch's own agencies. **No committee,
no floor vote, no legislation.** This is the least expensive of the three
asks in this repository, measured in process rather than in dollars — which is
why it is listed first.

The argument for this track is a process argument and holds under any
administration: commemorative observances are already directed by proclamation
and executive order as a matter of routine, in every administration of the
modern era. This asks only that one such observance be made **recurring**, and
be given a **form** — a reading — rather than a statement.

> **[DRAFTING NOTE]** Deliberately drafted without reference to any particular
> President's record or preferences. An EO justified by one administration's
> demonstrated appetite for commemorative proclamations is an EO that expires
> with that administration, and it reads as partisan to everyone else. The
> case here is that the ask is procedurally cheap and substantively small,
> which is true regardless of who holds the office.

## EXECUTIVE ORDER

**Restoring the Public Reading of the Founding Documents**

By the authority vested in me as President by the Constitution and the laws of
the United States of America, it is hereby ordered as follows:

### Section 1. Purpose.

On July 3, 1776, John Adams wrote that the anniversary of American
independence ought first to be commemorated, and then to be solemnized with
"Pomp and Parade." The celebration he named second has endured. The
commemoration he named first has not, in equal measure.

The founding documents of the United States were written to be read aloud, and
were so read to the public upon their adoption. A reading requires no
appropriation, no facility, and no personnel beyond those already present. It
is the least expensive act of civic instruction available to the Federal
Government, and it has fallen almost entirely out of Federal practice.

This order directs the executive branch to resume it.

### Sec. 2. Constitution Day Readings.

**(a)** On September 17 of each year, or on the next business day thereafter,
each executive department and agency shall conduct a reading aloud of the
Constitution of the United States, or of a substantial portion thereof, open
to the employees of that department or agency.

**(b)** The head of each department or agency shall determine the manner,
setting, duration, and portion selected, and may satisfy this section through
a single reading, multiple readings by component or location, or
participation in a joint reading with another department or agency.

**(c)** Participation by employees is voluntary.

### Sec. 3. Other Commemorative Days.

The head of each executive department and agency is encouraged, at the head's
discretion, to conduct comparable readings on Independence Day and on
Juneteenth National Independence Day, including readings of the Declaration of
Independence, the Emancipation Proclamation, General Order No. 3 issued at
Galveston, Texas on June 19, 1865, and the Thirteenth, Fourteenth, and
Fifteenth Amendments to the Constitution.

### Sec. 4. Materials.

The Archivist of the United States and the Librarian of Congress are requested
to make available, at no cost and in a form suitable for public reading, the
texts identified in sections 2 and 3 of this order.

### Sec. 5. No Additional Resources.

Nothing in this order shall be construed to require the expenditure of funds
beyond those already appropriated, the acquisition of any facility, or the
hiring of any personnel. Readings conducted under this order shall be
scheduled so as not to interfere with the performance of agency functions.

### Sec. 6. General Provisions.

**(a)** Nothing in this order shall be construed to impair or otherwise
affect:
1. the authority granted by law to an executive department or agency, or the
   head thereof; or
2. the functions of the Director of the Office of Management and Budget
   relating to budgetary, administrative, or legislative proposals.

**(b)** This order shall be implemented consistent with applicable law and
subject to the availability of appropriations.

**(c)** This order is not intended to, and does not, create any right or
benefit, substantive or procedural, enforceable at law or in equity by any
party against the United States, its departments, agencies, or entities, its
officers, employees, or agents, or any other person.

## Notes for the office receiving this

- **The natural recipients are the Domestic Policy Council and the Office of
  Public Liaison.** The ask is small, symbolic, costs nothing, and is
  difficult to characterize as partisan — a reading of the Constitution has no
  obvious opposing constituency.

- **Section 5 is the whole answer to the only likely objection.** An EO that
  costs nothing and displaces no agency function is hard to refuse on
  management grounds, so the text says so explicitly rather than leaving it to
  be inferred.

- **Section 2(c) matters.** Voluntary participation avoids any question of
  compelled ceremonial activity by Federal employees.

- **Section 3 is deliberately discretionary.** Making Juneteenth and
  Independence Day readings mandatory would multiply the objections without
  materially advancing the goal; Constitution Day is the day with an existing
  statutory hook and no competing observance practice.

- **This order does not depend on Track A or Track B**, and adopting it does
  not commit the administration to either.
